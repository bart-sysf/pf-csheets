Combat & movement

3 / 6 · AoOs, actions & maneuvers

Attacks of opportunity (AoOs)

Check in order: Does the enemy threaten your square with a melee attack? Does your action provoke? Can they make an AoO now? If yes, resolve one melee attack before the provoking action finishes.

Threatened area: usually adjacent squares, including diagonals. A typical reach weapon threatens 10 ft but not adjacent squares. Unarmed creatures generally do not threaten unless an ability says otherwise.

Usually provokes Usually does not
Leave a threatened square, including moving within threatened area Enter a threatened square; take a 5-foot step (if allowed)
Cast a spell normally; use a spell-like ability Cast defensively and succeed on concentration check
Ranged attack, including a ranged touch attack Melee attack, melee touch attack
Drink a potion; use a scroll; retrieve a stored item Drop a held item; draw a weapon; speak briefly
Stand from prone; reload many ranged weapons Withdraw from the starting square only; other squares may provoke
Most maneuvers against their target without relevant Improved feat 5-foot step; many maneuvers with relevant Improved feat

Movement exceptions: Acrobatics can avoid movement AoOs (check against opponent CMD, modifiers may apply). A 5-foot step requires movement no more than 5 ft in the round and suitable terrain. Withdraw protects only the starting square; cannot withdraw when blinded.

Limit: normally 1 AoO per round, even with many triggers. Combat Reflexes allows 1 + DEX bonus (minimum 1); only one AoO per opportunity, and movement from multiple threatened squares by one opponent is one opportunity. Flat-footed creatures normally cannot make AoOs; Combat Reflexes is an exception.

Turn & surprise basics

Regular turn: standard + move or full-round, plus swift/free actions. A standard can become a move. A 5-foot step forbids other movement. An immediate action uses your next swift and is unavailable while flat-footed.

Surprise round: occurs only when some, but not all, combatants are aware. Roll initiative; aware combatants take one standard or move action, plus free actions. Unaware combatants do not act. Everyone is flat-footed until their first turn unless an ability says otherwise. If everyone or no one is aware, skip the surprise round.

Common free actions

On your turn: cease concentrating on a spell; drop an item; drop prone; prepare ordinary spell components or a focus; speak briefly. Drawing ammunition is part of the attack, not a separate action. Free actions generally do not provoke, and the GM may limit how many are reasonable. Speaking can occur outside your turn.

CMB = BAB + STR mod + special size mod + other bonuses

Provocation: by default a maneuver provokes from its target. A relevant Improved feat or specific ability often removes this. If the target's AoO hits, subtract the damage dealt from your maneuver check. Individual maneuvers have limits and exceptions; check their text.

Cover vs. concealment

Cover is a physical obstruction in the attack’s path and normally gives +4 AC and +2 Reflex.

Concealment is obscured sight and gives a 20% miss chance after a hit. Total cover blocks line of effect; total concealment blocks sight, but you may attack a guessed square with a 50% miss chance. They can coexist.

Coup de grace: full-round action against a helpless foe; automatic critical hit. If defender survives, they make a Fortitude save DC 10 + damage dealt or die anyway. Provokes AoOs.

Dying and stabilizing

At 0 hp: disabled. At −1 to −(CON score − 1): unconscious and dying; lose 1 hp per round until stable. At −CON score: dead.

On your turn, make DC 10 Constitution check to stabilize, with a penalty equal to your negative hp total (e.g. −3 hp → check modifier −3; effectively DC 13 before CON modifier). Natural 20 succeeds. A successful DC 15 Heal check or any hp healing can stabilize you.

Rest and long-term care

Rest Natural recovery
8 hours 1 hp per character level; 1 point of ability damage per affected ability score.
24 hours complete bed rest 2 hp per character level; 2 points per affected ability score.
Successful long-term care (Heal DC 15) With 8 hours’ rest: 2 hp/level and 2 ability points per affected score. With 24 hours’ bed rest: 4 hp/level and 4 points. Healer provides 8 hours’ light activity.

Rest starts natural recovery once stabilized; a dying character first needs to become stable. Nonlethal damage heals on a different schedule.

Usually INT for wizard; WIS for cleric/druid/ranger; CHA for bard/paladin/sorcerer. Use your class feature for other casters. Constitution is not the default.
When casting... DC
Defensively (avoid casting AoO) 15 + 2 × spell level
Hit/damaged during casting 10 + damage taken + spell level
Continuous damage 10 + half last damage + spell level
Grappled or pinned 10 + grappler's CMB + spell level
Entangled (net/tanglefoot/similar) 15 + spell level
Affected by nondamaging spell That spell's save DC + your spell level
Vigorous / violent / extreme motion 10 / 15 / 20 + spell level

When do you actually roll?

  • Normally no check: ordinary spellcasting with no disruption. However, casting while threatened usually provokes an AoO; if it hits and deals damage, check concentration.
  • Choose defensive casting: make the check even if nobody hits you. Success prevents the casting AoO; failure loses the spell.
  • Actual disruption: damage during casting, grapple, entanglement or other listed interference calls for its check even without an AoO.
  • Already completed spell: later damage does not retroactively interrupt it. A spell requiring ongoing concentration has separate duration/maintenance rules.
Example: level 5 wizard, INT 18 (+4), casts a 3rd-level spell defensively: roll d20 + 9 vs DC 21, so 12+ succeeds. If instead hit for 8 damage while casting it, DC 21 also (10 + 8 + 3). If the AoO misses, there is no damage-based check.

Spellcasting and AoOs

Casting while threatened generally provokes (see page 3). A successful defensive cast avoids the casting AoO (DC on this page). Delivering a held melee touch spell does not itself provoke; a ranged touch attack usually does.

Spell-like abilities usually provoke and may be used defensively. Supernatural abilities generally do not provoke and cannot be disrupted; follow the specific ability text.

Timing matters: a spell with a casting time of 1 round or longer remains vulnerable to interruption until completion. If casting defensively and also subject to a separate disruption, resolve applicable checks separately.

Leveling up

★ House rule · Hit points
Both the player and GM roll the character’s Hit Die. Use the higher result, then add the CON modifier for HP gained that level (minimum 1 HP).

Update checklist

  • Add the new class level, Hit Die, BAB, base saves, class features, and spell progression.
  • Add skill ranks; check class-skill bonuses and maximum ranks.
  • Apply feats and ability-score increases when the new level grants them.
  • Recalculate HP, AC, attacks, CMB/CMD, saves, initiative, skills, spell DCs, and uses per day.
  • Review new spells, prepared spells, equipment, carrying capacity, and prerequisites.

Bonus stacking

Different bonus types usually stack. Same-type bonuses usually do not: use the highest. Dodge bonuses and most circumstance bonuses stack; untyped bonuses generally stack unless from the same source. Penalties usually stack unless stated otherwise.

Fast check: write the bonus type beside temporary effects. If two effects share a type, keep only the larger value unless the rule explicitly says they stack.
